UK Mortgage Types Explained

Residential Mortgage

The standard mortgage type for owner-occupiers. You can borrow up to 4.5× your gross income (some lenders offer 5–5.5× for higher earners). Minimum deposit is typically 5–10%, though 20%+ gives access to the best rates. Most lenders offer fixed-rate deals of 2 or 5 years, after which you move to a Standard Variable Rate (SVR) unless you remortgage.

Buy-to-Let (BTL) Mortgage

For landlords purchasing property to rent out. Most lenders require at least a 25% deposit (75% LTV) and assess affordability based on expected rental income — typically the monthly rent must cover 125–145% of the interest-only mortgage payment. Personal income is often assessed as a secondary check. BTL rates are usually 0.5–1% higher than residential rates.

Shared Ownership

A government-backed scheme where you buy a share (between 10% and 75%) of a property and pay subsidised rent on the remaining share to a housing association. You only need a mortgage for your share, so deposit requirements are much smaller. You can increase your ownership share over time (called staircasing) until you own 100%. Available on new-build and resale properties via registered providers.

Help to Buy: Equity Loan (Wales)

The Help to Buy Equity Loan scheme in Wales offers a government loan of up to 20% of a new-build property's value (on homes up to £300,000), meaning you only need a 5% deposit and a 75% LTV mortgage. The government loan is interest-free for the first 5 years. England's scheme closed in March 2023. Check the Welsh Government website for current eligibility and property limits.

What Is an Agreement in Principle?

An Agreement in Principle (AIP) — also called a Decision in Principle (DIP) or Mortgage in Principle — is a conditional statement from a lender confirming they would lend you a certain amount, subject to full application and property valuation. Most estate agents require one before accepting an offer.

  • Soft AIP: uses a soft credit search — does not affect your credit score. Most online tools issue these.
  • Hard AIP: uses a hard credit search — visible to other lenders for 12 months. Only a few providers use these upfront.
  • An AIP is valid for 60–90 days and does not guarantee a formal mortgage offer.
  • To get an actual AIP, approach a lender directly or use a whole-of-market broker.

UK Mortgage Rate Types

Fixed Rate

Your interest rate is fixed for an initial period (typically 2 or 5 years), giving payment certainty. After the fix ends, you revert to the lender's SVR unless you remortgage. As of mid-2025, 2-year fixes are around 4.5–5.0% and 5-year fixes around 4.2–4.75% for 75% LTV borrowers.

Tracker

Tracks the Bank of England base rate plus a set margin (e.g. Base Rate + 1%). Payments change when the base rate changes. Usually has no early repayment charges, making it flexible.

Standard Variable Rate (SVR)

The default rate you move to after a fix or tracker expires. Set by the lender and can change at any time — typically 1–2% above current fixed rates. Switching to a new deal before reverting to SVR almost always saves money.

Stress Testing

UK lenders must assess whether you can still afford the mortgage if rates rise by approximately 3% above the current rate. This affordability stress test, introduced by the FCA, means the maximum loan you qualify for on paper (income multiple) is often reduced in practice to meet the stress test. If rates are 4.75%, lenders test your ability to pay at ~7.75%.
